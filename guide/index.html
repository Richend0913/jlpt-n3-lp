What is JLPT N3?

The Japanese-Language Proficiency Test (JLPT) is a standardized test of Japanese for non-native speakers. It has five levels, from N5 (easiest) to N1 (hardest). The Japan Foundation and Japan Educational Exchanges and Services (JEES) run it.

N3 is the middle level. It bridges basic Japanese (N5 and N4) and the advanced levels (N2 and N1). At N3 you are expected to understand Japanese used in everyday situations to a certain degree. That means reading everyday texts and fairly simple articles written with common kanji, and following everyday conversations spoken at close to natural speed.

Exam format & sections

The exam is all multiple choice and is taken in three parts on the same day. It covers four skills:

  • Vocabulary: kanji readings, writing words in kanji, choosing the right word for the context, paraphrases and word usage.
  • Grammar: choosing the right grammar form, building sentences in the correct order and grammar in longer passages.
  • Reading: short, mid-length and long passages, plus finding information in notices or leaflets.
  • Listening: understanding the task, the key point, an overall summary, choosing what to say and quick responses.

How N3 is scored

Your result is reported in three scoring sections, each worth 60 points, for a total of 180. Vocabulary and grammar are combined into one section.

To pass, you need both: a total of at least 95 out of 180, and at least 19 out of 60 in every section. A high total does not make up for one section below 19.

How to prepare

  • Start with vocabulary. Vocabulary feeds every section. If you don't know the words, the reading passages and listening questions are much harder. Learn each word with its reading and an example sentence, not just a translation.
  • Study grammar in context. N3 grammar questions often test small differences between similar forms. Read example sentences aloud and notice when each form is used.
  • Read a little every day. Short news items, notices and graded readers help you build speed. On test day, time pressure in reading catches many people out.
  • Listen to natural-speed Japanese. Practice with conversations at close to real speed. Listening is a separate section with its own minimum, so don't leave it until the end.
  • Take timed mock tests. Do at least one full practice test under exam timing. Check your weakest section against the 19-point minimum.
  • Plan backwards from the exam date. Divide what is left to study by the weeks remaining and keep a steady pace instead of cramming.

FAQ

How difficult is JLPT N3?

N3 is the middle of the five JLPT levels. It bridges the basic levels (N5 and N4) and the advanced levels (N2 and N1). You need to read everyday texts written with common kanji and follow conversations at close to natural speed. Many learners find the step up from N4 to N3 bigger than they expected, mostly because of the jump in vocabulary and reading.

How long does it take to study for N3?

It depends on your starting point and how much time you study each week. There is no official figure. A common approach is to start several months before the exam and plan a steady weekly pace for vocabulary, grammar, reading and listening, so you have covered everything well before test day.

What score do I need to pass N3?

You need a total of at least 95 out of 180 points. You also need at least 19 out of 60 in each of the three scoring sections: language knowledge (vocabulary and grammar), reading, and listening. If you score below 19 in any one section, you fail even if your total is 95 or more.

How often is the JLPT held?

The JLPT is held twice a year, on the first Sunday of July and the first Sunday of December. Some countries and cities hold it only once a year, so check with the host institution where you plan to sit the exam.

What should I bring on exam day?

Bring your test voucher (admission ticket), the photo ID your test site requires, HB or No. 2 pencils, an eraser and a watch. Electronic devices, including smartwatches, are generally not allowed. Rules vary by country, so read the instructions from your local host institution before the day.
